\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is widely deployed for domain-specific question answering, but specialized government space agency documentation presents severe retrieval challenges marked by dense technical nomenclature, multi-step inter-mission relationships, and strict factual boundaries. We investigate whether Knowledge Graph (KG) augmentation provides a universal or task-conditional advantage over standard lexical and dense retrieval architectures under controlled local inference. We formulate and test the hypothesis that knowledge-graph augmentation provides a task-conditional benefit for entity-relational and temporally structured domain QA by supplying explicit, provenance-linked relationships, while excessive graph expansion can introduce irrelevant context and reduce answer quality. Evaluating across a frozen canonical 180-question test benchmark over 4,557 corpus chunks, sparse lexical retrieval (BM25+LLM) achieves superior lexical overlap (ROUGE-L 0.2915, coverage 0.4340) over Vanilla Dense RAG (0.2780, 0.3989) and KG-RAG (0.2736, 0.3921), demonstrating no significant overall advantage for KG augmentation on generic factoid retrieval ($p=0.403$). Conversely, on a targeted 36-question Aditya-L1 evaluation, the corrected KG-RAG pipeline achieves higher lexical overlap than Vanilla RAG (ROUGE-L 0.3859 vs.\ 0.2924). A controlled ablation shows that compact 1-hop graph augmentation is more effective than unrestricted 2-hop traversal, while corpus expansion can induce retrieval competition. On a separate controlled 62-question relational benchmark, a blinded human evaluation of 26 KG-required questions finds higher descriptive human ratings for corrected KG-RAG than Vanilla RAG on correctness, completeness, groundedness, and relevance. However, the corresponding confidence intervals include zero and no primary comparison remains significant after Holm correction; unsupported-claim rates are unchanged. Thus, the evidence supports a task-conditional and technically sensitive role for KG augmentation, but not a general claim of superiority.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) maintains over six decades of mission documentation, technical reports, and payload specifications. This corpus is characterized by dense inter-entity relationships (e.g., launch vehicles, payloads, orbit insertion trajectories, research centers) where standard retrieval systems frequently fail or produce misleading responses. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag} mitigates parametric hallucinations by retrieving relevant text passages prior to language model generation. However, standard RAG treats documents as unstructured textual fragments, lacking explicit awareness of multi-hop relational dependencies.

Recent literature proposes Knowledge Graph-augmented RAG (KG-RAG)~\cite{he2024gretriever, ma2024tog2, zhang2025kg2rag} to address this limitation by injecting structured entity-relation triples into prompt contexts. Nonetheless, a central methodological question remains unresolved: does KG augmentation confer a universal performance advantage across all query distributions, or is its utility conditional on specific relational and temporal query types?

In this paper, we conduct a rigorous experimental investigation into this question using the ISRO documentation domain. Rather than asserting universal superiority, we formulate and test the hypothesis that:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Knowledge-graph augmentation provides a task-conditional benefit for entity-relational and temporally structured domain QA by supplying explicit, provenance-linked relationships, while excessive graph expansion can introduce irrelevant context and reduce answer quality.}
\end{quote}

Our research contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A Provenance-Aware KG-Augmented Architecture:} We design an integrated RAG architecture combining rule-governed entity normalization, controlled relational vocabulary extraction, and FAISS dense retrieval with end-to-end evidence provenance.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Graph-Neighborhood Retrieval Methodology:} We demonstrate the critical difference between useful local 1-hop evidence and noisy unrestricted 2-hop graph expansion, showing how context budgeting and deduplication prevent prompt dilution.
    \item \textbf{Task-Conditional Empirical Evaluation:} Across a frozen canonical 180-question benchmark, a targeted 36-question Aditya-L1 set, a 40-question unanswerable dataset, and a 7-variant ablation, we establish that KG augmentation yields substantial gains for structured relational/temporal queries while not universally improving generic factoids or unanswerable rejection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Foundational and Dense Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
Lewis et al.~\cite{lewis2020rag} established the RAG paradigm by coupling Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) with sequence-to-sequence generation over Wikipedia. While effective for open-domain factoids, flat passage retrieval struggles when information is partitioned across heterogeneous documents. RAPTOR~\cite{sarthi2024raptor} introduced hierarchical clustering and recursive summarization to capture multi-scale context, though at substantial indexing and embedding cost.

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Integration with Language Models}
Pan et al.~\cite{pan2024unifying} surveyed LLM-KG unification frameworks, distinguishing between KG-enhanced generation, LLM-driven graph extraction, and bidirectional synergy. KAG~\cite{liang2024kag} and HippoRAG~\cite{gutierrez2024hipporag} demonstrated that structured schema traversal and Personalized PageRank simulate associative memory, improving multi-hop recall. G-Retriever~\cite{he2024gretriever} formulated graph retrieval via Prize-Collecting Steiner Trees. Think-on-Graph 2.0~\cite{ma2024tog2} and KG$^2$RAG~\cite{zhang2025kg2rag} demonstrated multi-hop reasoning by traversing graph subgraphs alongside text retrieval. However, these systems predominantly assume pre-existing structured knowledge bases (Wikidata, Freebase) or rely on cloud-hosted frontier LLMs for graph extraction.

\subsection{Evaluation Reliability and Abstention}
RAGAS~\cite{es2023ragas} and related frameworks propose automated reference-free metrics. However, recent findings emphasize that lexical metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) fail to reflect factual correctness or hallucination rates~\cite{rakin2024domainrag}. Rigorous evaluation requires explicit unanswerable benchmarking~\cite{peng2024graphragsurvey} and verified human judgment protocols.

\section{Proposed KG-RAG Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The proposed KG-RAG methodology is organized around four disciplined stages: deterministic query classification, controlled graph-neighborhood retrieval, multi-source evidence fusion, and evidence-grounded generation.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{kg_rag_system_architecture.png}
\caption{System architecture: document preprocessing, dual indexing (spaCy NER/syntactic parsing into NetworkX MultiDiGraph; MiniLM-L6-v2 into FAISS Flat L2), hybrid retrieval, and local Mistral-7B generation.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Deterministic Query Analysis}
To understand task-conditional retrieval behavior without non-deterministic LLM routing, queries are deterministically classified into four explicit categories:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Direct Factual Lookup (FACTUAL):} Single-entity lookups (e.g., definitions, acronyms, standalone quantities).
    \item \textbf{Entity-Relational QA (RELATIONAL):} Direct single-step relationships connecting entities (e.g., launch vehicle, developer center, payload designation).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Hop Relational QA (MULTI\_HOP):} Compositional bridging questions spanning two or more relation hops.
    \item \textbf{Temporal/Chronological QA (TEMPORAL):} Date identification, mission sequencing, duration, and milestone ordering.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Controlled Graph-Neighborhood Retrieval}
Unrestricted graph expansion rapidly causes context explosion. We implement controlled graph traversal:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Default Depth (1-hop):} For factual, relational, and temporal queries, only immediate outgoing and incoming typed edges connecting detected seed entities are traversed.
    \item \textbf{Constrained 2-hop:} Traversal expands to 2-hop neighbors only when the query classification layer detects compositional multi-hop syntax, strictly capped by an edge budget.
    \item \textbf{Provenance Tracking:} Every retrieved triple records its source document ID, chunk ID, URL, and graph traversal path.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dense + KG Evidence Fusion}
We implement a 7-step evidence fusion procedure governed by configuration parameters tuned exclusively on development data:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dense Retrieval:} Top-$k$ passage chunks ($k=3$) are retrieved via FAISS exact L2 search.
    \item \textbf{Entity Matching:} Query entities are mapped to canonical normalized labels.
    \item \textbf{Controlled KG Retrieval:} 1-hop or constrained multi-hop triples are fetched.
    \item \textbf{Deduplication:} Triples whose exact propositional content is already subsumed within retrieved dense chunks are pruned.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Ranking:} Dense passages and unique KG triples are ranked according to similarity scores.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Context Budget:} A strict budget of 1,200 tokens is enforced, preventing 2-hop retrieval from bloating context size.
    \item \textbf{Context Formatting:} Structured triples and passages are serialized with explicit provenance headers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Grounded Generation}
Answer generation is performed using an instruction-tuned local language model under deterministic decoding (temperature 0.0). The prompt strictly restricts the model to the supplied evidence:
\begin{quote}
``Answer ONLY using the facts explicitly stated in the supplied context. If evidence is insufficient, answer EXACTLY: I don't know.''
\end{quote}
Chain-of-thought tokens are suppressed; the system outputs the final factual answer along with explicit supporting chunk IDs and graph paths.

\section{ISRO Corpus and Knowledge Graph Construction}
\label{sec:corpus_kg}

\subsection{Corpus Ingestion and Metadata Provenance}
Documentation was crawled from official ISRO and ISSDC portals covering mission releases, launch records, instrument handbooks, and annual reports. Documents were cleaned of boilerplate and segmented into 512-token chunks with 128-token overlap. Every chunk preserves structured provenance metadata: \texttt{document\_id}, \texttt{title}, \texttt{mission}, \texttt{source\_type}, \texttt{source\_url}, \texttt{page}, and \texttt{section}. The canonical corpus comprises 339 documents and 4,557 chunks (mean length: 9,874 characters). The expanded corpus includes 58 targeted Aditya-L1 chunks, yielding 4,615 chunks.

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Construction & Controlled Vocabulary}
Entities are extracted using spaCy's \texttt{en\_core\_web\_lg} pipeline augmented with an ISRO domain entity ruler covering missions, payloads, launch vehicles, facilities, and dates. Entity alias normalization maps synonymous surface forms (e.g., ``aditya l1'', ``aditya-1'' $\to$ ``Aditya-L1''; ``pslv c37'' $\to$ ``PSLV-C37''; ``suit'' $\to$ ``SUIT'').

Relations are extracted via dependency parsing over syntactic paths between co-occurring entities within two syntactic hops. Raw predicates are mapped to a controlled vocabulary: \texttt{HAS\_PAYLOAD}, \texttt{LAUNCHED\_ON}, \texttt{LAUNCHED\_BY}, \texttt{DEVELOPED\_BY}, \texttt{OPERATES\_AT}, \texttt{HAS\_OBJECTIVE}, \texttt{CARRIES}, \texttt{REACHED}, \texttt{OBSERVES}, and \texttt{HAS\_PART}. The resulting NetworkX MultiDiGraph contains 2,193 nodes and 4,655 typed edges for the canonical corpus (2,237 nodes and 5,137 edges expanded).

\subsection{Dense Vector Indexing}
Chunks are encoded with \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} (384 dimensions) and indexed using an exact FAISS Flat L2 index (27,545 total vectors).

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Benchmark Suites}
We evaluate across four frozen benchmark datasets and one human-evaluation subset:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Frozen Canonical ISRO-QA (180 Questions):} A deterministic split of 200 questions (20 dev, 180 test) spanning Tier 1 (Factoid, $N=90$), Tier 2 (Relational, $N=54$), and Tier 3 (Timeline, $N=36$).
    \item \textbf{Targeted Aditya-L1 Evaluation (36 Questions):} A researcher-constructed targeted evaluation focusing on the Aditya-L1 solar observatory mission.
    \item \textbf{Curated Unanswerable QA (40 Questions):} A benchmark with ground-truth $\text{answerable} = \text{false}$ across five unanswerable failure modes.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Relational QA (62 Questions):} A frozen benchmark constructed around explicit direct, single-relation, two-hop, and multi-relation cases, with 26 questions labeled KG-required and 36 labeled KG-not-required.
    \item \textbf{Blinded Human Evaluation (26 KG-required Questions):} The final human evaluation compares Vanilla RAG, corrected Structured KG-RAG, and BM25+LLM using 1--5 ratings for correctness, completeness, groundedness, and relevance, plus a binary unsupported-claim indicator.\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Metrics and Claim Restrictions}
Automated metrics are reported strictly as lexical and abstention proxies: ROUGE-L, reference-token coverage, Exact Match (EM), and IDK Rate. Factual correctness is not inferred from lexical overlap.

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}
All comparisons use question-level paired evaluations. Primary statistical tests report:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
    \item 95\% empirical bootstrap confidence intervals (1,000 resamples).
    \item Cohen's $d_z$ effect size and rank-biserial correlation ($r_{rb}$).
    \item Holm-Bonferroni correction for multiple primary comparisons.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Compute Statement}
Experiments were conducted using local inference with a quantized instruction-tuned language model (\texttt{mistral:7b-instruct-q4\_K\_M}).

\begin{enumerate}

\subsection{Human Factual Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:res_human}

To complement lexical diagnostics, we conducted a blinded human evaluation on the 26 KG-required questions from the controlled 62-question relational benchmark. Each question was evaluated for Vanilla RAG, corrected Structured KG-RAG, and BM25+LLM using 1--5 ratings for correctness, completeness, groundedness, and relevance, together with a binary unsupported-claim indicator. Candidate answers were mechanically validated against the authoritative blinded generation package before analysis: all 78 evaluations matched exactly, all score fields were complete and valid, and no question/label pair was duplicated. Paired comparisons use the same Wilcoxon, bootstrap, effect-size, and Holm-correction protocol described above. The evaluation was performed by a single annotator, so the results are exploratory rather than an inter-annotator reliability estimate.

\subsection{Final Human Factual Evaluation}
\label{sec:res_human_final}

The final blinded evaluation provides the strongest factual-quality evidence in this study. On the 26 KG-required questions, corrected Structured KG-RAG received higher mean ratings than Vanilla RAG on all four dimensions: correctness (2.962 vs. 2.115), completeness (2.962 vs. 2.154), groundedness (3.500 vs. 3.346), and relevance (3.615 vs. 3.154). BM25+LLM scored 2.269, 2.269, 3.692, and 3.308, respectively.

The descriptive KG-RAG--Vanilla differences were +0.846 for correctness, +0.808 for completeness, +0.154 for groundedness, and +0.462 for relevance. However, none of the corresponding 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals excluded zero. The Wilcoxon $p$-values were 0.117, 0.152, 0.673, and 0.109, respectively; after Holm correction they were 0.435, 0.435, 0.673, and 0.435. Thus, the human evaluation does not establish a statistically significant overall factual-quality advantage.

Unsupported-claim rates were 38.46\% for both corrected KG-RAG and Vanilla RAG, compared with 30.77\% for BM25+LLM. Consequently, the experiment provides no evidence that the corrected graph pipeline reduces unsupported claims. The result is therefore classified as a partial improvement: corrected KG-RAG is descriptively better across the four rated dimensions, but the evidence is statistically uncertain and does not support a general superiority claim.

The final evaluation also resolves an important implementation issue identified in the earlier human study. The original frozen evaluation queried a graph artifact whose entity/path representation was not aligned with the provenance-linked triples used to construct the relational benchmark. The corrected diagnostic and final evaluation use the canonical provenance-linked graph and preserve path/evidence identity through generation and annotation. The earlier negative human result therefore remains a valid result for the old implementation, while the new result evaluates the corrected pipeline rather than retroactively replacing the old measurement.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our experimental findings substantiate four major scientific conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Task-Conditional Advantage:} KG augmentation is not universally superior. On broad factoid benchmarks with well-represented lexical keys, BM25+LLM outperforms graph-dense hybrids. KG-RAG achieves decisive gains specifically on entity-dense, multi-hop relational queries (Aditya-L1: +9.35 pp ROUGE-L over Vanilla RAG, $p=0.0098$).
    \item \textbf{Graph Granularity and Hop Constraints:} Compact 1-hop subgraphs provide the ideal balance between factual grounding and prompt economy; unrestricted 2-hop traversal introduces distractors that degrade performance.
    \item \textbf{Corpus Distraction Dynamics:} Unfiltered corpus expansion degrades dense retrieval on unrelated queries. Entity graph filtering acts as an anchoring mechanism, providing resilience against retrieval competition.
    \item \textbf{Abstention vs.\ Semantic Distraction:} Lexical IDK rate reflects prompt-constrained abstention rather than hallucination suppression. On unanswerable queries, graph context can occasionally introduce semantic distraction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We acknowledge several important limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lexical Metric Proxies:} Automated ROUGE-L and token coverage do not establish factual correctness; human factual evaluation remains pending.
    \item \textbf{Researcher-Constructed Targeted Set:} The 36-question Aditya-L1 dataset was curated by the authors and does not constitute independent external validation.
    \item \textbf{Human and Retrieval Annotation Dependency:} Rigorous factual correctness and retrieval quality claims require completed human annotations.
    \item \textbf{Corpus Scale:} The domain corpus is focused on ISRO documentation; scaling to broader multi-agency archives requires approximate graph indexing.
    \item \textbf{Local Generator Sensitivity:} Generator performance is subject to quantization choices, though decoding parameters were held strictly constant.
    \item \textbf{Unanswerable Query Handling:} KG augmentation does not improve unanswerable query rejection and can introduce minor semantic distraction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an empirical investigation of Knowledge Graph-augmented RAG on ISRO documentation under controlled local inference. The frozen canonical benchmark shows that KG augmentation does not provide a universal advantage, while targeted and controlled relational experiments indicate that compact, provenance-linked graph evidence can improve retrieval and answer quality for relation-rich questions. The final blinded human evaluation provides descriptive support for this interpretation, with corrected KG-RAG scoring higher than Vanilla RAG on correctness, completeness, groundedness, and relevance, but without statistically significant differences after multiple-comparison correction and without a reduction in unsupported claims. The resulting conclusion is therefore one of task-conditional potential rather than demonstrated general superiority. All benchmark datasets, execution scripts, generation traces, and annotation packages are released to support reproducible domain RAG research.
